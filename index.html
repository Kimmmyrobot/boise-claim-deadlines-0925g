Manage Accident Deadlines With a Layered Calendar

An accident file can contain several kinds of dates, and they do not all carry the same consequence. A treatment appointment, insurer response request, video-retention window, rental return date and statutory filing deadline belong on one calendar but need different labels and verification.

01

Layer one: health and recovery dates

Enter medical appointments, therapy, diagnostic tests, prescription refills and work-status reviews. These dates protect continuity of care and help explain the recovery timeline. If an appointment changes, preserve the cancellation or rescheduling reason rather than deleting the original entry.

02

Layer two: evidence preservation

Video can be overwritten, vehicles repaired and witnesses become harder to locate. Record when a preservation request was sent, who received it and what response arrived. Note inspection opportunities and storage deadlines. A preservation date is not proof that evidence exists, but it documents a timely effort to prevent avoidable loss.

03

Layer three: insurance administration

Track claim reporting, document requests, statement appointments, inspections, rental authorization, proof-of-loss forms and offer dates. Distinguish a requested response date from a policy condition or legal deadline. Ask for written clarification when the consequence of missing a date is unclear.

04

Layer four: legal limitation periods

A general rule found online may not resolve the date for a particular claim. Government entities, minors, death, delayed discovery, multiple jurisdictions or special defendants can create different notice and filing questions. Negotiating with an insurer does not necessarily pause a statutory period.

05

Layer five: litigation events

If a lawsuit is filed, court rules and orders can govern service, disclosures, discovery, expert work, mediation and trial preparation. Missing a litigation deadline may create consequences different from missing an adjuster’s preferred response date. Court dates should identify the source document and responsible person.

06

Verify the calendar early

A Boise injury claim lawyer can calculate case-specific deadlines and identify notice rules that a general checklist may miss. The calendar should be reviewed while evidence can still be gathered, not only when a date appears close.

07

Use colors for source, not importance

Color-code dates by source: provider, insurer, evidence custodian, contract, statute or court. Attach the letter, policy, rule or order supporting each entry. A layered calendar is reliable when another person can trace every date to its source and see which dates still require confirmation.

Schedule reminders before the actual date, with longer lead time for records, expert review or court filings. A reminder on the deadline is not a planning system. Give each calendar item an owner and a completion field, then require written confirmation when responsibility moves to another person. This reduces the risk that everyone assumes an insurer, provider or lawyer is monitoring the same date when the engagement or request never actually assigned that task. Review the calendar after every important letter, court notice or coverage decision so a new obligation does not remain buried in correspondence.
